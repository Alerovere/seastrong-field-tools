# Seagrass Structure and Bathymetry

## Overview

This page describes how to measure the **structure** of seagrass meadows and the **depth profile** along the wave-attenuation transects. The structure is used to parameterise seagrass drag in the XBeach models; the depth profile gives the bed along which wave attenuation is measured.

The layout, header, file names, safety and effort common to all habitats are on the [Field transects](field_transects.md) page.

The method is **non-destructive** (nothing is collected) and designed to be quick in the water: shoots are counted afterwards from photos, and blade measurements are taken in place.

---

## Transect layout

The transect follows the [common layout](field_transects.md#transect-layout-all-habitats): a **30 m** tape laid on the bottom from **sensor 1 (0 m, seaward)** to **sensor 2 (30 m, landward)**. On seagrass:

- fill in the [common header](field_transects.md#on-every-transect), and note the tape positions where the meadow visibly changes (bare sand, patchy, continuous; change of dominant species);
- work by snorkelling, preferably around **slack water**, when the leaves stand more upright and the depth changes slowly.

## Depth profile and cover (every 2 m)

This is the [common bed profile](field_transects.md#bed-profile-and-vertical-datum), with a cover photo added. Swim along the tape and stop **every 2 m**, from 0 m to the 30 m mark and then **5–10 m beyond it**. Always include a stop at each sensor. At each stop:

1. Read the **water depth** with a depth gauge or a metered stick held vertically on the bottom, next to the tape.
2. Note the **time** of the reading. The depths are later corrected for the tide using the pressure-sensor record.
3. Take **one vertical photo** of the bottom, straight down, from about 1 m above it, with the tape in the frame. These photos give the seagrass **cover** along the transect (bare, patchy, continuous) and the zone boundaries for the model.

---

## Quadrats

Sample three positions along the transect: **front** (2.5 m), **centre** (15 m) and **back** (27.5 m), the same positions used on the mangrove transects. At each position, place **three replicate 25 cm × 25 cm quadrats** (a rigid frame of known size), **9 quadrats per transect**:

- in a **line across the tape** at the position mark: one **at the tape** (centre), one **2 m to the left** and one **2 m to the right**;
- always **on seagrass**: if the planned spot is bare sand, move the quadrat along the same line to the nearest seagrass and record the distance moved. Do not look for the densest or most attractive patch. Bare areas are accounted for by the cover photos taken every 2 m, not by the quadrats.

To keep the work feasible, the effort per quadrat is small; the replicates, not the number of shoots per quadrat, give the spread. See the [workflow for two snorkellers](#workflow-for-two-snorkellers) for who does what and how long it takes.

At each quadrat, in this order:

1. **Canopy height:** with the metered stick, read the height of the undisturbed canopy at **3 points** around the frame.
2. **Photo 1, canopy:** a vertical photo of the whole frame, straight down, before touching the leaves. It gives cover, species composition and a record of the quadrat.
3. **Photo 2, shoot bases:** push the leaves aside with one hand and take a vertical photo close to the bottom, so that the **shoot bases** inside the frame are visible. The shoots are counted later on this photo to obtain **shoot density**. If one photo cannot show the whole frame, take two (one per half) and note it.
4. **Measure 5 shoots** of the **dominant species in place**, without cutting or pulling them: take the shoots nearest to marked points on the frame (so the sample is not biased towards long leaves). Note the other species present.

### On each shoot (in place)

Use a ruler fixed to the slate (mm scale) and a simple plastic caliper, and write the readings directly on the slate.

| Measurement | How |
| --- | --- |
| Number of blades | Count the blades on the shoot |
| Blade length | Length of the **longest blade**, from the base to the tip, held straight along the ruler |
| Blade width | Width of the same blade at mid-length, against the ruler |
| Blade thickness | On **one shoot per quadrat**: hold all the blades of the shoot together at mid-length, measure the thickness of the stack with the plastic caliper (jaws closed gently, without squashing), and divide by the number of blades |

Thickness is measured because published values of blade thickness are not available for all seagrass species; together with length and width, it allows a correction for blade flexibility in the models (see [Notes for modelling](#notes-for-modelling)).

### In the lab

- Count the shoots inside the frame on each Photo 2 → shoots per 25 cm × 25 cm → **shoots per m²** (× 16).
- For each position (front, centre, back), report the **mean and spread of the three replicates** for density, canopy height, blades per shoot, blade length, width and thickness.
- Estimate **percent cover** from the photos taken every 2 m along the tape.

## Workflow for two snorkellers

The survey is designed for **two snorkellers** working together. Snorkeller A is the **recorder**: depths, shoot measurements and the slate. Snorkeller B is the **photographer**: frames, photos, canopy height and blade thickness. Two quadrat frames are used, so that B can prepare the next quadrat while A is measuring the current one.

| Step | Snorkeller A (recorder) | Snorkeller B (photographer) | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Set-up | Lay the tape from sensor 1 to sensor 2 and on for 5–10 m; write the header of the slate (site, transect, date, sensor positions) | Hold the 0 m end; note GPS position and bearing (from the boat or the surface) | ~10 min |
| 2. Depth and cover | Swim along the tape; every 2 m read the depth (stick or gauge) and write depth and time | Follow A and take the vertical photo at each stop, tape in view | ~15–20 min |
| 3. Quadrats (front, centre, back) | At each quadrat: measure 5 shoots (blades, length, width) and write them on the slate; then move to the next frame | Place the frame; read canopy height (3 points); take Photo 1 and Photo 2; measure one thickness stack and call it to A; place the second frame at the next quadrat | ~5 min per quadrat, ~45–50 min for 9 |
| 4. Check and recover | Check that the slate is complete (9 quadrats, all stops) | Check on the camera that all photos are there and sharp; recover frames | ~5 min |
| | Recover the tape together | | ~5 min |

**In total, about 1.5 hours in the water per transect** for two snorkellers, in good conditions (good visibility, little current, 1–2 m depth). Allow more in strong current or poor visibility, and plan no more than two transects per day for the same pair.

## Notes for modelling

### From the field data to XBeach vegetation input

Seagrass is usually represented as a single vegetation section in XBeach, with:

| XBeach input | From the field data |
| --- | --- |
| Vegetation height (ah) | Canopy height (mean of the 3 readings per quadrat), or an effective blade length that accounts for flexibility (see below) |
| Stem width (bv) | Mean blade width |
| Density (N, per m²) | Shoots per m² (from Photo 2) × mean number of blades per shoot |

Within each zone along the transect, the density is scaled by the **cover** from the photos every 2 m, so that bare and patchy stretches are not given the density of the quadrats. The mean of the three replicate quadrats at the front, centre and back gives the values for the corresponding parts of the transect; the spread between replicates shows how uncertain they are.

### Flexibility

XBeach's vegetation module treats stems as rigid, whereas seagrass blades bend under waves. Flexibility is either absorbed into the calibrated drag coefficient, or accounted for with an **effective blade length** (Luhar and Nepf, 2011), which needs blade length, width, thickness and stiffness. Thickness is measured in the field; stiffness is taken from the literature where available. Where stiffness is not available for a species, flexibility is absorbed into the calibrated drag coefficient. Use the same choice for all transects of the same species, so that calibrated drag coefficients can be compared.

### Depth relative to canopy

Wave attenuation by seagrass depends strongly on the ratio between canopy height and water depth. Depths along the transect must be corrected for the tide and referred to the common vertical datum (see [Bed profile and vertical datum](field_transects.md#bed-profile-and-vertical-datum)). The other notes common to all habitats are on the [Field transects](field_transects.md#notes-for-modelling-all-habitats) page.

### Consistency

Shoot density from photos can differ from density counted by hand. Use the same photo method at all sites, so that any bias is consistent and is absorbed in the same way by the calibrated drag coefficient.

## Field form and post-site report

The header, the depth and cover sheet, the sheets for the 9 quadrats and the post-site report are in one form: {download}`Seagrass field form, v1.0 (PDF) <_static/forms/seagrass_field_form_v1.0.pdf>`.

- **In the water:** print pages 1–5 on waterproof paper, or copy them onto the slate. Each quadrat has its own block (front, centre and back, each with left, centre and right).
- **After the survey:** type the data and the post-site report (page 6) into the same PDF, the same day.
- **Fill it in with Adobe Acrobat Reader** (free, [download it here](https://get.adobe.com/reader/)). In Acrobat Reader the **Transect ID** is filled in automatically from study area, date and corridor, and appears on every page, and the form fields are saved correctly. Other viewers (Mac Preview, browsers, phone apps) may not fill in the Transect ID or may not save the fields.
- Save the filled form with the data, named `<transectID>_field_form.pdf`, and put the data download link in section B of the report.

## Why we do this in SEASTRONG

In **WP1, Task 1.2 / WP4**, the structure of the seagrass meadow between the two pressure sensors is used to explain the measured wave attenuation and to parameterise seagrass drag in the XBeach models. The depth profile gives the bed along which waves propagate, needed both to interpret the sensor records and to build the model profile.
